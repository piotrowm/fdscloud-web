Modelling Smoke Control Systems in FDS

What needs to be represented when analysing mechanical or natural smoke control: extract, make-up air, activation, openings and the outputs used to judge performance.

The system is more than an extract rate

Specifying only an exhaust flow can miss important physics. Replacement air, opening geometry, flow paths, pressure effects and activation sequence can influence smoke-layer stability and local velocities. The model should represent the operating concept being assessed.

1. Define the fire and system sequence

Document the design fire, detection or activation time, fan start-up, damper states and any time-dependent changes. If the analysis assumes immediate full extract, that assumption should be explicit.

2. Represent make-up air

Air removed from a space must be replaced. The location, area and velocity of incoming air can affect plume behaviour and smoke-layer mixing. Door openings and mechanical supply should therefore be modelled consistently with the scenario.

3. Select the right outputs

Typical outputs include smoke-layer behaviour, visibility, temperature, gas concentrations and velocity at critical locations. The appropriate criteria depend on the engineering objective and applicable project requirements.

4. Check sensitivity

Potential sensitivity variables include fire location, HRR, open-door state, extract performance, make-up-air configuration and mesh resolution. A robust study explains why the selected scenarios cover the important combinations.

Important: FDS results should be interpreted in the context of model assumptions, uncertainty and the acceptance criteria used for the project. A colourful contour plot is not by itself evidence of system performance.
