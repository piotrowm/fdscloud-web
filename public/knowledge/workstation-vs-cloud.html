FDS Workstation vs Cloud: When Does Cloud Compute Make Sense?

Local compute and cloud compute solve different constraints. The useful comparison is workload, turnaround time and capacity — not a claim that one is universally better.

Costs and runtimes below are synthetic placeholders for page development only. They are not market prices, purchasing advice or measured FDSCloud results.

Where local compute is strong

A capable workstation can be economically attractive for continuous, predictable workloads and gives the engineer direct control over local resources. For small models and non-urgent jobs, moving compute elsewhere may provide little practical benefit.

Where cloud changes the equation

Cloud capacity becomes interesting when demand is irregular, several independent scenarios need to run at once, project deadlines make queue time expensive, or buying hardware for occasional peak demand is difficult to justify.

The FDSCloud question

Rather than asking whether cloud is always faster, our future benchmarks will ask: for this FDS model and this deadline, what compute configuration provides a useful balance of turnaround time and actual cost?
